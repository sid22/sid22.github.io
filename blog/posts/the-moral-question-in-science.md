# The Moral Question in Science

*This is the first article I ever published — it appeared in* The LNMIIT Gazette *(Nth Degree, 2016), our college magazine, when I was a first-year student. I'm reproducing it here, unchanged, as a small time capsule.*

---

The year is 1919; one of the greatest pioneers in human history Albert Einstein has proposed The Theory of Relativity, arguably the most important theory in modern physics. Come to 14th-September-2015, scientists working at Laser Interferometer Gravitational-Wave Observatory (LIGO) have physically sensed the gravitational waves proposed in the Theory of relativity, thereby giving more credibility to the Theory of relativity which has now become the cornerstone of modern physics.

While the press was swathed by articles highlighting the importance of this seemingly humongous discovery, most people were reading the *LIGO for dummies* guide tucked in the corner. Modern science has leap frogged beyond the understanding of a common man. The contentious issue is that modern day discoveries and inventions aren't in tune with the needs of the human society in general. Skeptical critics balk at the rhetoric of the scientific community to advance humans into space beyond earth. Critics rightly argue that when 795 million people don't have enough to eat, space is a scientific fantasy which satiates elitist mindset of scientists. Even in our country, seconds after the success of Mars mission, critics from all over the world harshly judged India's space ambitions. Some like New York Times laced their judgment with envy.

The chasm between the elite scientists and everyday person is getting wider. Most people find it difficult to relate to the modern day advances in science. The general populace feels lost in the growing cacophony of notions put in the scientific community. This has fostered hidden paranoia, and in some cases resentment towards the discoveries and inventions perceived as elitist. The current developments like Artificial Intelligence has fanned this paranoia even more, people are getting increasingly wary of the new technologies which have tangled them in an intricate web spanning from their social life to their medical records. In absence of any rational mediating body between the scientific community and general populace, this void is filled by media agencies which often feed on the hysteria. Some people even believe that the rise of the conservative parties all over the world can be linked to the inner desire of people to return to 'good old days' without getting assaulted by the battery of liberal arguments advocating science.

Human civilization has never seen the rapid transformation we are experiencing now. It took thousands of years for us to invent and make use of wheels and fire. Today the famous Moore's law stating computers double their capabilities every 2 years is in effect. As stakeholders in the scientific community we must strive to uphold human sentiments. Rather than basking in self-glory, the scientific community should consciously involve the general public in their achievements. This will bolster the general perception and further incentivize scientific achievements.

> "The saddest aspect of life right now is that science gathers knowledge faster than society gathers wisdom." — Isaac Asimov
